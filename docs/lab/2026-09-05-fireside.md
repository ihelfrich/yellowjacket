# 2026-09-05 — FIRESIDE: a song from public-domain culture, on instruments derived from it

Ian's redirection at 01:20: not objects on a desk but "public domain sounds,
signals, short clips of famous speeches, songs, movies, popular culture" —
derive the instruments from those, make a song now, real objects later when
the microphone arrives. Everything here is public domain or CC0; the edge is
recordings published before 1926 and works of the US federal government.

Score: `scripts/compose-fireside.mjs`. Master: `scripts/master-take.mjs`.
Cards: `docs/lab/cards/`. Output 2:33, 440 rendered notes, rendered in 20 s.

## 1. What the material forced: spectral cards

The modal fitter cards what decays. A vowel, a band chord, a buzzer burst and a
time-signal tone do not decay while they sound, and on all of them the fitter
returned zero or one junk mode (§3 of `2026-09-05-found-instruments.md`). So
the spec's second phase came forward: `js/instrument/spectral.js` reads a
stationary segment as its spectral peaks — at the harmonics of a known f0 for
voiced sound — under a **harmonic envelope**, the line through the peaks
themselves. Decay times are an assumed Q, and the card says so
(`damping.assumed: true`); a release, when supplied, measures them per partial.
`family.js` then keeps the envelope fixed while the comb moves with pitch: a
voice's formants stay where the mouth put them. The tests pin this: a
synthetic vowel transposed an octave up or down keeps its loudest partial under
the 500 Hz formant, not on the fundamental.

`js/analysis/pitch.js` is a YIN tracker (difference function, cumulative-mean
normalisation, parabolic refinement, voicing) with steady-run detection. On a
220 Hz tone with ±4 Hz vibrato it reads 220.0 with the vibrato intact.

## 4. What the numbers caught before anyone listened

The first master read −3 dBFS RMS at a 2 dB crest in the intro: a full-scale
drone. Stems showed why: the bowed pad and bass came out at +13 and +9 dB RMS,
fourteen times full scale, because a bowed twenty-partial card sustains far
louder than any strike, and a peak-based bus normalisation let them win by
being continuous. Fix: each bus is normalised to a stated RMS over its
sounding samples (voice −16, bass −20, pad −22, drum −18 …), then panned and
summed. The mix went from a 2 dB crest to 16.8 dB. A second pass found the
outro six decibels louder than the verses (the final tone); trimmed.

Final master: −15.0 LUFS integrated, −1.0 dBTP; sections within 1.7 dB of one
another (RMS −15.5 to −17.2 dBFS), crest 8.7–15.3 dB.

## 5. Not yet, and honestly

- Nobody has listened. The measurements say it is balanced and not broken;
  they do not say it is good.
- The blown voice's breath noise and the assumed Q on the vowels are the two
  most audible assumptions; a release measured from a real sung note would
  replace the second.
- The band card is a chord, not a horn: no isolated note in the 1921 mix was
  clean enough to fit. A struck or bowed note from a pre-1926 solo recording
  would be the next card to find.
- Movies: the pre-1930 films are public domain but their soundtracks are
  music-and-effects mixes; the same dense-material problem applies.
