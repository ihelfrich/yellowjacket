# Yellowjacket DSP and sound-design audit

Scope: every requested signal path as of 2026-07-27. “Professional” means deterministic across browsers, bounded in-band error, controlled aliasing/latency, and live/offline renders that null except where explicitly stochastic.

## PART 1 — HONEST QUALITY AUDIT

1. **EQ and fixed filters — audible and measurable.** `BiquadFilterNode` peaking and shelving shapes compress near Nyquist; capping frequency at `0.95 * Nyquist` prevents invalid values, not cramping. Replace the EQ with deterministic Orfanidis/Vicanek matched-response peaks and matched-z shelves, tested at every supported rate. The high-pass is also wrong: two Q=0.707 sections are not a fourth-order Butterworth. Use the pole-derived Q pair 0.541196/1.306563. Fixed-Q dehum notches miss drifting mains and sidebands; add a narrow frequency tracker and redesign stable SOS coefficients per block.

2. **Compressor — audibly unpredictable and latency-bearing.** `DynamicsCompressorNode` has a fixed 6 ms pre-delay, while detector/release behavior remains user-agent territory. `renderNodesStage()` allocates input length with no latency/tail compensation, so heads and tails can be lost. Static makeup predicts neither gain reduction nor crest factor. Replace it with shared JS/WASM feed-forward compression: explicit lookahead, peak/RMS blend, quadratic knee, stereo link, and program-dependent dual release. Pad then trim renders; derive makeup from measured long-term reduction or expose it manually.

3. **Denoise — audibly fragile.** Quietest-frame stationary profiling plus a blurred binary mask classifies low consonants/decays as noise and leaves isolated bins as burbling musical noise. Independent channel jobs also move the stereo image. Replace it with minima-controlled recursive noise tracking, decision-directed a-priori SNR, and Wiener gain `ξ/(1+ξ)` (or Ephraim–Malah log-MMSE). Asymmetric multi-frame and frequency smoothing plus a psychoacoustic floor removes mask chatter; derive one linked gain field from mid/maximum channel energy. Cost: roughly two STFT passes.

4. **Limiter and loudness normalization — audibly unsafe.** A 5 ms attack inside 5 ms lookahead cannot reach target before the transient. Final per-sample `min(gain, ceiling/peakNow)` restores sample-peak compliance with abrupt envelope corners; one-pole release makes clustered peaks pump. Derive required gain from oversampled peaks, build attack by backward/sliding minimum over lookahead, then take the windowed minimum of shaped release ramps, with hold and two-stage recovery. Loudnorm must measure → gain → true-peak limit → remeasure and iterate once; today limiting changes the promised LUFS unchecked.

5. **Oversampling and true peak — measurable, audible on hard material.** There is no oversampling anywhere. The limiter constrains samples only; its emergency gain corners add wideband modulation, and saturation would alias immediately. The “4x” true-peak estimator is only an eight-tap, three-phase interpolator with edge clamping, despite the contract naming a 48-tap four-phase FIR. Replace both with one ITU-R BS.1770/EBU-style 4x polyphase FIR used by metering and limiting. Run nonlinear drive at 4x with half-band/polyphase filters; offer 2x economy mode, never 1x above mild drive.

6. **WAV quantization and ASR resampling — measurable; audible in fades and harmful to recognition.** `encodeWav()` clips and rounds directly. Add deterministic-seeded TPDF dither at ±1 LSB before 16-bit quantization; keep 24-bit dither optional and disable noise shaping by default. Report pre-encode overs instead of silently clipping. The Whisper path is worse: 48 kHz→16 kHz lands on every third input sample, so “linear interpolation” becomes unfiltered decimation and everything above 8 kHz aliases into speech bands. Move a ratio-aware Kaiser-windowed polyphase sinc resampler into a worker; derive length from transition width (about 320 taps for 48→16 kHz, 7.2 kHz passband, ≥80 dB stopband) and preserve duration/timestamp mapping exactly.

7. **Splices and sample edges — plainly audible, plus timeline drift.** A fixed 6 ms equal-power crossfade suits uncorrelated sources, not two points in one recording: it can bump/cancel and smear plosives. Each overlap shortens output, but `editedTime()` subtracts only cuts, so captions drift. Search ±3 ms for the minimum-discontinuity/zero-crossing join, then use a correlation-normalized complementary raised-cosine fade of adaptive 1–10 ms. Return overlaps in a splice map for caption timing. Audition fades edges; sequencer voices do not. Add 1–3 ms per-voice trigger/stop fades.

8. **Machine render parity and headroom — audible and structural.** Triggers play the whole sample, overlapping without choke, voice limits, envelopes, or master safety. Offline ends at `totalSec`, truncating tails that live playback keeps; the two contexts can also resample differently. Per-step locks cannot safely automate shared track gain without changing older voices. Move velocity, pan, pitch, bounds, and envelope to per-voice nodes; reserve strips for mixer moves. Define choke/stealing, render tails, fix one project rate, and true-peak-limit before 16-bit FREEZE.

9. **Secondary processors and analysis — audible in edge cases; otherwise measurable.** Gate has no lookahead, hold, or hysteresis, so it clips attacks and chatters; implement a linked RMS expander with lookahead, separate open/close thresholds, hold, and range. De-ess uses fixed absolute band level, so bright loud vowels trigger like sibilance; use a high-band/full-band ratio detector or STFT spectral de-esser. Beat tracking is fixed to 4/4, strongly biased toward 120 BPM, and reports auto-period confidence even when BPM is pinned; expose octave candidates, recompute pinned confidence, and treat downbeat as unknown below threshold. Spectrogram column decimation samples frames instead of pooling them, so short transients can disappear; max-pool display columns.

## PART 2 — FRONTIER PROPOSALS FOR SOUND DESIGN

1. **Expressive sampler voice and LOCK grammar.** This unlocks pitched chops, tight drums, bass lines, reverse hits, choke groups, ratchets, and deterministic pump. Extend compiler events from `{tSec, track, gain, pan}` to a tagged trigger plus per-voice `{velocity, start, duration, rate, reverse, attack, hold, release, choke}` and strip automation segments. Compile sidechain as attack/hold/release gain segments triggered by another track’s events, not an audio detector. Cost is under 2% CPU for ordinary voices; cap at 32 voices. `compileWindow()` and `compileRender()` remain the sole event producers; `Sequencer` consumes the same event schema live and offline.

2. **Per-track filter and anti-aliased drive.** This makes each track an instrument instead of a sample launcher. Use a topology-preserving-transform state-variable filter (LP/BP/HP, resonance, key/velocity envelope) followed by 4x oversampled ADAA tanh or cubic saturation and DC blocking. Insert it between per-voice gain and the existing strip gain/pan; LOCK emits sample-accurate AudioParam-style ramps. Budget 1–3% desktop CPU per active driven track at 4x, roughly double on low-end phones; cap full-quality drive to four simultaneous tracks and freeze beyond that.

3. **Tape and SP-style character block.** Add wow/flutter, head bump, soft saturation, dropout, resample-rate reduction, bit crushing, and vinyl/noise as independently lockable components. Use a modulated fractional delay with cubic interpolation, filtered stochastic LFOs, oversampled hysteretic saturation, then anti-alias low-pass before sample-rate reduction; dither the crusher. Seed all noise from project/event IDs so bounce matches live. Budget 2–5% per tape instance and below 1% for lo-fi alone. Implement as another track insert and reuse it as LIVE punch FX.

4. **One send rack: convolution space plus tempo delay.** Partitioned convolution turns dry chops into rooms, plates, springs, and impossible resonators without eight convolvers. Sum per-track sends into one stereo bus; use uniform 1024-sample FFT partitions with a 128-sample direct head and overlap-save, then a feedback delay network or damped ping-pong delay after it. Budget 3–8% desktop CPU for a four-second stereo IR, 15–30% on phones; only one convolution bus. Ship a small provenance-checked set of project-owned/CC0 IRs, accept user WAV IRs, and provide algorithmically generated IRs as the license-free default. Extend strips with `sendReverb/sendDelay`; LOCK automates sends.

5. **Granular clip mode.** This unlocks clouds, stutters, pads, microloops, and semantic-word textures. A no-SAB AudioWorklet can own a copied, bounded track sample after assignment; schedule Hann grains of 20–120 ms with 2–8 overlaps, independent position/pitch/density jitter, transient-aware start snapping, and deterministic PRNG. Cap at 32 live grains and two granular tracks: about 3–8% desktop CPU per track and 10–25% on phones, plus one extra PCM copy. Add `track.playMode='grain'` and grain parameters to trigger/lock events; use the identical processor for offline FREEZE.

6. **CONFORM as a derived-asset service.** Integrate signalsmith-stretch WASM in a module worker, never on the trigger path. Pre-render and cache by `{sampleId, sourceBpm, targetBpm, semitones, formantMode, quality}` in OPFS; transfer channel arrays, process one job at a time, and keep the old asset playing until replacement is atomic. Provide `FORMANT: SHIFT | PRESERVE | OFFSET`; preservation separates a cepstral spectral envelope from excitation, shifts excitation, then reapplies or offsets the envelope. Budget 0.2–1.0× audio duration on desktop and 1–4× on phones, background-only, with a 2x-PCM working-memory check. `track.sample` points to the derived asset while ClipRef remains immutable.

7. **LIVE rolling resampler with spectral hold.** Put the two-bar ring entirely inside one AudioWorklet—messages carry gestures, never PCM—so GitHub Pages needs no SAB. Variable-rate cubic/Lagrange read heads implement reverse, scrub, stutter, octave, and tape stop; a 1024/256 phase-vocoder hold adds frozen vowels and harmonic smears. Raw buffer effects cost 1–3% CPU; spectral hold costs 5–12%, so allow one spectral instance. Place it after the MACHINE mix/send buses and before the true-peak safety stage; gesture recording compiles controls onto the same pattern-time axis, and RESAMPLE captures the post-FX stream as a new source.

8. **UNMIX as stem-aware material, not an insert.** Separation unlocks vocal chops over the original bed, instrumental-only resampling, stem swaps, and stem-triggered ducking. Run the chosen two-stem Demucs ONNX model as preparation, enforce mixture consistency so stems sum back to the source, and join overlapping inference chunks with sqrt-Hann crossfades before writing each completed chunk to OPFS. The honest budget is a 172–316 MB model and minutes per song on WebGPU, much longer on single-thread WASM, but zero inference CPU during playback; never keep Whisper resident beside it. Store `DerivedAsset {kind:'stem', sourceId, role, sampleRate, channels}` and assign it through the existing `track.sample` boundary so compiler and sequencer require no stem-specific path.
